The best caption style for short video

Short answer: The caption style that works best is the one people can read in a glance: a bold sans-serif, large, with an outline or background bar for contrast, three to six words at a time, placed mid-frame. Use one highlight colour for the spoken word, and save animation for emphasis.

Rules that hold up

  • Weight beats decoration: bold, simple letterforms read fastest on a phone.
  • Contrast: an outline, shadow or bar keeps text readable over any background.
  • Short groups: three to six words per caption suits fast talking-head video.
  • One accent colour: highlight the current word or key words, not everything.
  • Consistent placement: the eye learns where to look.

Match the style to the content

ContentStyle
Fast tips, hooksWord-by-word, bold, highlight colour
Calm explainers, coursesTwo-line subtitles, no animation
Stories, vlogsSmaller, softer look, lower contrast bar
Brand contentBrand font and one brand colour

A simple default

If you do not want to think about typography, pick a high-contrast style with a strong weight, keep the captions large enough to read at arm's length, and sit them in the safe zone. Use one style for a series so viewers recognise you. Word-by-word highlighting helps when you want the eye to follow the speech; a static two-line block is calmer for longer explainers. Questions people ask

Is word-by-word always better?

No. It suits fast, punchy clips. For longer explanations, two-line subtitles are easier to follow.

What colour should captions be?

White or yellow with a dark outline is the safest. Pick a brand colour for highlights only.
