Accessible captions: a practical checklist

Short answer: Accessible captions are accurate, readable and in sync. Fix every word, use high contrast and a large size, keep lines short, show who's speaking when it changes, and note important sounds like [music] or [laughs]. Offer a caption file where the platform supports it.

The checklist

  1. Accuracy: proofread every caption; automatic captions are a first draft.
  2. Contrast: light text with a dark outline or bar, or the reverse.
  3. Size: large enough to read on a phone at arm's length.
  4. Line length: one or two short lines.
  5. Timing: on screen while the words are spoken, not before or long after.
  6. Speakers: label a new speaker when it isn't obvious.
  7. Sounds: add cues like [music] or [door slams] when they matter.
  8. Motion: flashy animations can be hard to follow; use them sparingly.

HandCut's captions are built primarily for English speech. Other languages work only when your iPhone has an on-device speech model for them, and the app tells you before you generate if yours does not.

Burned-in and closed

Burned-in captions always show; closed captions let viewers adjust size and contrast. Offering both covers most needs. See burned-in vs closed captions.

Questions people ask

Are auto captions accessible enough?

Not without review. Errors in names, numbers and key terms change meaning. Always proofread.

Should I caption music videos?

Caption lyrics if they matter, and note [music] otherwise.
