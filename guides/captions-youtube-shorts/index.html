How to add captions to YouTube Shorts

Short answer: Burn captions into the Short so they show on mute in the Shorts feed, and optionally upload a matching .srt file in YouTube Studio for viewers who use the CC button. HandCut makes both on your iPhone.

Step by step

  1. Edit the Short in 9:16. Import with Add Video and set Canvas to 9:16. Shorts can be up to three minutes, so trim any dead air first.
  2. Cut the pauses. Pro's Auto-Cut can remove silences and tighten pacing before you caption. See removing silence.
  3. Generate and check captions. Tap Generate Captions, then read through and fix any words.
  4. Export the video. Tap Export Video for a burned-in version.
  5. Optional: export the SRT. In the caption tray, tap Export .srt and add the file in YouTube Studio under Subtitles.

Burned-in or CC: which should you use?

Burned-in captions carry the style and are always visible, which matters in a feed that autoplays muted. A caption file is searchable text and can be turned off by viewers. Many channels use both, but don't show viewers two sets at once: if you burn captions in, consider leaving YouTube's automatic captions as the CC track only.

Bottom of the Shorts screen

The Shorts player overlays the title, channel name and subscribe button along the bottom and actions on the right. Keep captions mid-frame.

Questions people ask

Does YouTube auto-caption Shorts?

YouTube can generate automatic captions as a CC track, but they're hidden unless the viewer turns them on, and they're not styled.

Can I export an SRT from my iPhone?

Yes. HandCut's caption tray has Export .srt. It counts as an export, like a video export.
