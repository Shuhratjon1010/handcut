Subtitles on iPhone: the complete guide

Short answer: Open the clip in HandCut, tap Generate Captions, fix any word you want, pick a style, and tap Export Video. The subtitles are transcribed on your iPhone, timed to each spoken word, and burned into the exported video with no watermark.

iPhone's Photos app can trim a video but can't add subtitles to it, and Clips only captions what you record inside Clips. If you already have the video, you need an editor that can listen to the audio and write the text for you. For the quick steps only, see how to add subtitles to a video on iPhone. This guide goes further and uses HandCut because its subtitles are made on the phone itself: the audio isn't uploaded, and you don't need an account.

Step by step

  1. Import the clip. Open HandCut, tap Add Video and choose the video from Photos. Clips you recorded on any camera app work.
  2. Generate the subtitles. Open Auto Captions and tap Generate Captions. HandCut runs Apple's on-device speech recognition and keeps the timing of every word.
  3. Proofread. Tap any caption to fix a word, a name or punctuation. Fixing the text keeps the word timing, so the highlight still lands on time.
  4. Choose how they look. Open Style to pick a preset, a caption look and a text animation, then drag the captions where you want them on the frame.
  5. Export. Tap Export Video. Pick the resolution (up to 4K) and the video saves to Photos with the subtitles burned in.

Subtitles or captions: is there a difference?

People use the words interchangeably on social video. Strictly, subtitles are the spoken words as text, and captions can also describe sounds. On TikTok, Reels and Shorts, what people want is the same thing: the words on screen, in sync with the voice. HandCut makes that, timed word by word.

Burned-in subtitles vs a subtitle file

Burned-in subtitles become part of the picture, so they show everywhere the video is played, including in feeds that autoplay on mute. A separate .srt file is better when a platform like YouTube lets viewers turn subtitles on and off. HandCut does both: Export Video burns them in, and the caption tray has Export .srt and Import .srt. See exporting an SRT file.

Language

HandCut's captions are built primarily for English speech. Other languages work only when your iPhone has an on-device speech model for them, and the app tells you before you generate if yours does not.

Questions people ask

Can I add subtitles to a video on iPhone without an app?

Not to a video you already recorded. Photos has no subtitle tool. You need an editing app; HandCut does it on the phone with no account.

Are the subtitles accurate?

They come from Apple's on-device speech recognition, which is very good on clear English speech. Always read them through once: names and brand words are the usual misses, and you can tap any word to fix it.

Will there be a watermark?

No. HandCut never adds a watermark to an export.
