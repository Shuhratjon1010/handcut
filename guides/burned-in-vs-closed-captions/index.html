Burned-in captions vs closed captions: what's the difference?

Short answer: Burned-in (open) captions are drawn into the video pixels and always show. Closed captions are a separate text track, like an .srt file, that viewers can switch on and off. Short-form feeds favour burned-in; YouTube and course platforms also benefit from a caption file.

Accessibility

Both help deaf and hard-of-hearing viewers. A closed track lets viewers pick size and contrast in their player; burned-in captions should use high contrast and a large size.

Making both on iPhone

In HandCut, Export Video burns captions in, and Export .srt in the caption tray writes a caption file from the same words and timing. If you only need the file, turn burn-in off in the export settings.

Questions people ask

Are subtitles and closed captions the same?

Close. Subtitles carry dialogue; closed captions can also describe sounds like [music]. Online, people often use both words for the same thing.

Which is better for SEO?

A caption file on YouTube is text YouTube can read. Burned-in text is pixels. For short-form feeds, retention from burned-in captions usually matters more.
