Hinglish captions on iPhone: what works

Short answer: No single-language speech recogniser handles Hinglish perfectly. In HandCut, generate captions in English (or Hindi, if your iPhone has the on-device model), then fix the mixed words by hand. Find and replace speeds up words you use often. For a fully Hindi script, import an SRT made elsewhere.

Pick the recogniser by what you say most

  • Mostly English with some Hindi words: generate in English. English words come out well; Hindi words may be misheard and need retyping in Roman script.
  • Mostly Hindi: only possible if your iPhone has an on-device Hindi model. HandCut tells you before generating if it doesn't.

Fast fixing workflow

  1. Generate captions.
  2. Play through once and tap each wrong word to fix it; timing stays.
  3. Use find and replace for words you say in every video (your name, show name, catchphrases).
  4. Keep lines short so mixed-script text stays readable.

Roman or Devanagari?

Most Hinglish creators caption in Roman script because viewers read it fast. You can type Devanagari into any caption if your audience prefers it.

Be honest with your viewers

If captions are machine-made and lightly checked, small errors slip through. For brand deals and education content, proofread fully.

Fixing mixed lines by hand

Mixed Hindi and English in one sentence is hard for any speech model. After you generate captions, read every line and fix names, English words that were written in the wrong script, and Hindi words that came out as English lookalikes. Import an .srt if you already have a better transcript. The HandCut interface stays in English. This is slower than a clean single-language take, but it is the honest way to publish mixed speech.
